## Structure of the Volume

### Part I: Democracy as a Mode of Living
* **Chapter 1: The Promise, the Premise, and the Balance Sheet of Democracy**
  * Republican liberty as non-domination (Pettit/Skinner) vs. mere non-interference.
  * The six carrying costs of self-government.
  * Historical Audit: The trial of the Arginusae generals (406 BCE) and Socrates as sole dissenting prytanis.

### Part II: Pathologies of Civic Decay
* **Chapter 2: The Epistemic Environment, Educational Neglect, and the Cannibalization of Truth**
  * The trap of credentialism (Sandel/Bourdieu) vs. civic dannelse.
  * The Göttingen mathematicians under Nazi purges (1933) and Hilbert's reply.
  * The Danish Folk High School (*Folkehøjskole*) model (Grundtvig).
* **Chapter 3: Daily Participation Beyond the Quinquennial Mirage**
  * Cognitive bandwidth taxes and time poverty (Mullainathan & Shafir).
  * Athenian sortition hardware (*kleroterion*, *pinakia*, Boule).
  * Institutional Citizen Assemblies and the Citizens' Initiative Review (CIR).
* **Chapter 4: Rights, the Rule of Law, and Law as Shield vs. Weapon**
  * Civil death and universal patrimonial liability (Napoleonic Code Art. 1911).
  * Modern Second Chance insolvency mechanisms (EU Directive 2019/1023).
  * Solon’s *Seisachtheia* (594 BCE) and the uprooting of the *horoi*.
* **Chapter 5: Pluralism, Disagreement, and the Civic Courage of the Middle Ground**
  * Agonism (legitimate adversaries) vs. Antagonism (existential enemies).
  * Late Roman Republic collapse: Gracchi agrarian reforms (133--121 BCE) to Caesar.
  * The Spanish Moncloa Pacts (1977) and the Courage of the Golden Mean.
* **Chapter 6: Responsibility to Descendants and the Intergenerational Compact**
  * The Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace (*Gayanashagowa*): Rule of the Seventh Generation.
  * The Jefferson--Burke debate: The earth in usufruct vs. intergenerational partnership.
  * Four constitutional locks against generational deficit looting.

### Part III: Systemic Capture and Institutional Demolition
* **Chapter 7: The Equality Paradox, the Poison of Ressentiment, and the Strongman's Siren Song**
  * The Tocqueville Paradox and Girardian mimetic desire.
  * Demagogy after Pericles: Cleon and Aristophanes' *The Knights* (424 BCE).
  * The Nordic test: Aksel Sandemose’s Law of Jante (*Janteloven*).
* **Chapter 8: Money, Parties, and Politics as Public Infrastructure**
  * Post-democracy (Colin Crouch) and the iron law of oligarchy.
  * Tammany Hall patronage vs. the British Chartists' *People's Charter* (1838).
  * Democracy Vouchers and Political Action Groups (PAGs) as clean public infrastructure.
* **Chapter 9: The Outsider Vote, the Protest Ballot, and the Recycled Agitator**
  * The protest vote: legitimate grievances vs. the "need for chaos" (Petersen et al.).
  * The fatal recycling of the coup plotter: Munich 1923, Venezuela 1992/1994.
  * The Augustan facade: dismantling constitutional checks behind electoral rituals.
* **Chapter 10: The Gatekeepers: Strategic Failure of Elite Self-Interest**
  * The March on Rome (October 1922) and King Victor Emmanuel III's abdication of duty.
  * Franz von Papen hiring Hitler (January 1933): *«Wir haben ihn uns engagiert»*.
  * The 50% abstention desertion from below vs. democratic quarantine (Finland 1932, Belgium 1937).
* **Chapter 11: The Manufacture of Will in the Digital Age**
  * Quintus Cicero's election manual (64 BCE) and the Dreyfus Affair rage press (1894).
  * Walter Lippmann's pseudo-environment and Bernays' invisible government.
  * Cambridge Analytica, microtargeted *dark posts*, and the Liar’s Dividend in generative AI.
* **Chapter 12: Autopsy of a Demolition: The Venezuelan Tragedy**
  * The 1950--1990 sanctuary for European immigrants and regional exiles.
  * Chávez's psychological warfare dossier, PDVSA decapitation, and mutation into narco-state.
  * El Helicoide, La Tumba, the Tascón/Maisanta employment blacklists, and 10 million exiled.

### Part IV: The Architecture of Democratic Recapitalization
* **Chapter 13: Teaching the Reading of Intention and Authoritarian Smoke Signals**
  * Victor Klemperer's philology of tyranny (*LTI*) and Umberto Eco's 14 traits of Ur-Fascism.
  * Carl Sagan's Baloney Detection Kit.
  * Juan J. Linz's four behavioral litmus criteria for identifying authoritarian actors.
* **Chapter 14: Defending Democracy Without Becoming a Target: The Exposure Ladder**
  * The honest worker's dilemma (Brecht's *Galileo*) and Timur Kuran's preference falsification.
  * Programmable CBDCs vs. the non-negotiable defense of physical cash.
  * German Federal Constitutional Court (BVerfG 2009) ban on black-box electronic voting machines.
  * The Exposure Ladder (Steps 1--3) and collective action thresholds (Granovetter).
  * Historical precedents: Zola's *J'accuse*, Danish October 1943 Jewish rescue, Euromaidan 2014.
* **Chapter 15: Constitutional Reversibility: Designing for the Knave and the Return Ticket**
  * David Hume and James Madison (*Federalist 51*): Designing institutions for the knave.
  * Ulysses bound to the mast (Jon Elster): Rational constitutional precommitments.
  * The Roman 6-month dictatorship (Cincinnatus) vs. Sulla and Caesar's perpetual mandate.
  * Weimar Article 48 emergency decree abuse vs. Bruce Ackerman's supermajoritarian escalators.
* **Chapter 16: Verified Recoveries, the Fallacy of the Foreign Savior, and the Open Door**
  * The myth of the external savior vs. geopolitical realpolitik.
  * Verified historical turnarounds: Denmark's March 23, 1943 election under occupation (89.5% turnout), West Germany's 1949 *Grundgesetz* (militant democracy), Spain's 1977--78 transition, South Africa's 1994 CODESA.
  * The contemporary debated case: El Salvador under Nayib Bukele (audit matrix).
  * Open letter to a disillusioned youth, master table of mechanisms, and civic checklist.
